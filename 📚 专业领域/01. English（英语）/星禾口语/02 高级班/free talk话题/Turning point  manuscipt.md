
Another Turning Point for Me

Before I started university, I was a quiet and cautious person. I was always kind of scared of trying new things because I worried that I might fail or embarrass myself. I would rather stay in my comfort zone than speak up in class, join an activity, or meet unfamiliar people. I admired classmates who could express their ideas confidently, but I had always been too shy to do the same. As a result, I often missed chances that might have made my life more interesting.

Another turning point for me was when I began to play badminton at university. At first, I joined simply because some classmates invited me to the court. I thought it would be useful to have some exercise after a long day of study, and I was also tired of spending too much time alone in my dormitory. People say that sport can change not only your body but also your mind. I did not completely believe this at first, but badminton gradually proved it to me.

In the beginning, I was not good at all. I could not hit the shuttlecock accurately, and I felt nervous whenever other people watched me play. However, my teammates were friendly and patient. They taught me basic skills, encouraged me after mistakes, and celebrated every small improvement with me. Little by little, I learned that making mistakes was not something to be ashamed of; it was a necessary part of becoming better. For the first time, I felt that I could face a challenge instead of avoiding it.

Playing badminton made me more active and positive. I became willing to start conversations, join group activities, and take responsibility for things that I had once been afraid of. I even began to volunteer in class and try tasks outside my usual routine. Surprisingly, many things went more smoothly after that, not because life suddenly became easy, but because I became braver and more prepared to act.

Looking back, badminton was more than just a sport for me. It gave me confidence, friendship, and a new attitude toward difficulties. It taught me that a small decision can become a turning point, opening the door to a better version of myself.
